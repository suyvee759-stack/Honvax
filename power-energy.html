WHERE HONVAX FITS

Power procurement ranges from repeatable hardware to high-consequence electrical equipment.

Grid and project packages should be assessed according to the actual technical requirement, approval route and after-sales responsibility.

Commodity hardware may be relatively straightforward. Transformers, switchgear and other higher-risk equipment require stronger evidence, testing and support planning.

WHAT THE RFQ SHOULD DEFINE

Information needed before a reliable sourcing decision.

01

Ratings and system conditions

Voltage, current, fault level, frequency, load profile and installation environment.

02

Standards and project specifications

Applicable IEC, SANS, utility or project-specific requirements.

03

Approved brands and equivalents

Whether alternatives are permitted and what evidence is required for approval.

04

Testing and certification

Type-test reports, routine tests, factory documentation and client approval requirements.

05

Local content and support

Local-content obligations, commissioning, spares, warranty and after-sales responsibility.

SOURCING DECISION

The right supply route depends on more than factory price.

Stronger candidates

  • Planned grid-material and cable-accessory packages with clear specifications
  • Repeatable hardware with sufficient quantity and a practical verification route
  • Project-power requirements with defined duty, lead time and support scope
  • Packages with adequate approval, production and shipping time

Requires early caution

  • Utility-approved products without a confirmed approval route
  • High-consequence equipment lacking required type-test or project evidence
  • Emergency items required before import lead time is realistic
  • Equipment dependent on rapid local commissioning or service
