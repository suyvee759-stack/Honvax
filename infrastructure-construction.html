WHERE HONVAX FITS

Import value often comes from the package, not one commodity item.

Individual steel or construction items may not justify import freight and handling. A coordinated package can be more attractive when volume, fabrication value and project planning support the economics.

Material grade, fabrication, coating, documentation, packaging and container loading all affect the delivered outcome.

WHAT THE RFQ SHOULD DEFINE

Information needed before a reliable sourcing decision.

01

Material and dimensions

Grade, section, wall thickness, dimensions, tolerances and applicable standards.

02

Fabrication

Approved drawings, welding requirements, inspection points and fabrication tolerances.

03

Protection

Galvanizing, paint system, coating thickness and surface preparation.

04

Quality evidence

Mill certificates, welding records, inspection reports and traceability.

05

Delivery constraints

Packaging, container loading, site access, local-content requirements and required delivery sequence.

SOURCING DECISION

The right supply route depends on more than factory price.

Stronger candidates

  • Consolidated specification-led material packages
  • Fabricated items where Chinese manufacturing adds value beyond commodity price
  • Projects with enough volume to support freight and inspection economics
  • Packages planned early enough for production, coating and shipping

Requires early caution

  • Small commodity quantities readily available locally
  • Items with high freight cost and little fabrication value
  • Urgent site requirements that cannot wait for import lead time
  • Packages subject to unconfirmed local-content or site-installation obligations
