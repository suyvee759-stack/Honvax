HOW WE WORK

What happens after you send an RFQ

The exact scope depends on the requirement and agreed commercial arrangement. The stages below show the practical work that may be required.

01

Requirement review

Establish whether the requirement is clear enough to source and whether importing is worth investigating.

Detailed service →

Client inputs

  • RFQ or BOQ
  • Product descriptions and quantities
  • Drawings, datasheets and specifications
  • Required standards or approved brands
  • Delivery location and required date

HONVAX review

  • Check scope and quantity completeness
  • Identify conflicting or missing information
  • Confirm critical ratings, materials and dimensions
  • Review standards and equivalent-product rules
  • Screen order size, timing and import practicality

Possible output

  • Clarification list
  • Initial sourcing-fit assessment
  • Defined sourcing scope
  • Recommendation to proceed, revise or source locally
Decision point

If the requirement is unsuitable for import or lacks critical information, supplier enquiry should not begin yet.

02

Supplier development and enquiry

Build a viable China supply route around the clarified requirement, rather than a generic supplier list.

Detailed service →

Basis of enquiry

  • Clarified technical scope
  • Required quantities and delivery timing
  • Documentation and certification needs
  • Known commercial constraints

HONVAX work

  • Identify relevant manufacturers and suppliers
  • Check manufacturer or trader status
  • Review production capability and relevant experience
  • Confirm MOQ, lead time and payment expectations
  • Issue consistent enquiries and follow up clarifications

Possible output

  • Viable China supply route
  • Internally shortlisted suppliers
  • Comparable supplier offers
  • List of unresolved technical or commercial issues
Decision point

If suitable capability or compliance cannot be demonstrated, HONVAX should not force a quotation.

03

Technical and commercial evaluation

Compare what is actually offered—not only the headline factory price.

Detailed service →

Technical review

  • Compare the offer with the specification
  • Record compliance, deviations and exclusions
  • Review drawings, datasheets and available evidence
  • Confirm materials, ratings, dimensions and accessories

Commercial review

  • Compare price scope and exclusions
  • Review lead time, validity and payment terms
  • Check warranty and delivery responsibilities
  • Consider packaging, inspection, freight and landed cost

Possible output

  • Technical and commercial comparison
  • Recommended supply option
  • Assumptions and outstanding questions
  • Formal quotation or proposed procurement scope
Decision point

The offer proceeds only when scope, compliance, risk, delivery basis and commercial terms are sufficiently clear.

04

Order, quality and delivery execution

Coordinate the agreed supply package after the purchase order or formal service scope is confirmed.

Detailed service →

Before production

  • Confirm purchase scope and specification
  • Confirm approved drawings and documents
  • Agree milestones, quality checks and hold points
  • Define required production and shipping records

During execution

  • Follow agreed production milestones
  • Coordinate technical queries and document submissions
  • Coordinate inspection where included
  • Record deviations and required corrective actions

Possible output

  • Order and production updates
  • Inspection records where included
  • Commercial and export documents
  • Shipping, delivery and close-out coordination
Decision point

Shipment release follows the agreed approval, inspection and documentation requirements for the order.

BETTER INPUTS, BETTER QUOTATIONS

What improves quotation quality

Complete inputs reduce avoidable assumptions and make supplier offers easier to compare.

DOCUMENTS

Technical information

BOQ, specifications, drawings, datasheets, standards, approved brands and acceptable-equivalent rules.

COMMERCIAL

Purchasing information

Quantities, delivery location, required date, preferred delivery basis and relevant payment requirements.

PROJECT

Decision context

Tender status, bid closing date, approval process, local-content requirements and acceptable alternatives.

COMMERCIAL BOUNDARY

Where the initial review ends

The initial review is limited and intended to decide whether further sourcing work is justified.

Initial assessment may include

  • China supply feasibility
  • Likely sourcing route
  • Major risks and missing inputs
  • Recommendation to proceed or source locally

Formal work may include

  • Supplier development and factory engagement
  • Detailed quotation and technical clarification
  • Supplier information agreed for the engagement
  • Inspection, expediting, export and delivery execution

Supplier contacts, original supplier quotations, HONVAX purchasing prices and detailed sourcing work are not part of the initial assessment.
